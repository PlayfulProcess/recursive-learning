The Lab Years — Rulebook

3-5 players, about 90 minutes, pass-and-play on one device. First playable — play it here. This is a game with stated assumptions, not a forecast: see "Assumptions" near the end. Arguments, not people — no villains, and real companies or people never appear in it.

Setup

  1. Choose 3-5 players. Cast the start year with a coin draw over 2017-2026 (The Fork's own draw primitive; see Credits). The later the year, the more compute, capability and safeguard progress is already on the board, from real, dated history — each card with its source, face up before anyone's first turn.
  2. Deal (or choose) one role per player, and one secret objective per player. Deal (or agree) a bloc per player, for the Nation objective. Two World cards are dealt face down and nobody looks at them (see "The hidden World").

A round is one year

  1. Compute +1 for everyone (more for a CEO, or after a publish bonus).
  2. Draw event card(s): 1 while the race is under 3, 2 under 6, 3 at 6 or more. From 2027, RSI cards are shuffled into the event deck. One clue card is also revealed, publicly, each year.
  3. Each player takes a full turn of 3 actions, in order: Research (draw a card), Build (spend 1 compute + a card: capability +1, company value +1), Release (spend 2 compute: capability +2, race +1, company value +2 — opens a response window first), Secure (bank matching, or wild, cards toward one of the 4 safeguards — 2 cards for international agreement or public understanding, 6 for alignment or containment: those two are the ones the tree actually reads), Publish (a card face up: it counts for Open science, and gives everyone a compute bonus next year), Lobby (agreement +1, no card needed), Trade (freely within your bloc; across blocs needs consent), or a role's power.

Dual-use, and the response window

Every research card can be spent for capability (Build) or banked toward a safeguard (Secure) — never both. That choice is the drama: a card that would finish a safeguard is also a card that would ship a step of capability.

When a player Releases a frontier model, before it resolves, an eligible red-teamer may Audit it (capability step -1) and an eligible whistleblower may blow the whistle on it (race rise -1), each once. Both, either or neither may respond.

RSI: does it fire?

An RSI ("recursive self-improvement") card only fires if global capability has reached the game's threshold; below it, the card fizzles and the game says why. When it fires, it resolves through the same belief tree the recursive.eco film explainer uses (rendered live): is alignment secured? is containment secured? A fully secured safeguard reads "yes"; an unstarted one reads "no"; a partly built one is cast, with odds set by how far along it is (The Fork's own draw primitive — never a coin flipped by hand, never a new one written for this game).

AlignmentContainmentResult
yesyesProceed — the best ending; the game ends and scores.
yesnoRegulate — the race is capped; the game continues.
noyesContain — capability freezes and the race is pushed back; the game continues.
nonoCatastrophe — the game ends; everyone loses.

The game ends at the end of 2035, or the moment an RSI card resolves to Proceed or Catastrophe.

The hidden World

Two cards were dealt face down at setup, and nobody — not even the interpreter, beyond one private peek — knows both, for certain, before the reveal. Together they decide how RSI actually behaves.

Takeoff (pick 1 of 3)

WorldWhat it does
SingularityWhen RSI fires, capability jumps hard within that year, and the tree resolves at once.
Automated researchOnce RSI fires, capability (+2) and the race (+1) keep climbing on their own, every remaining year; the tree only resolves at the end of the game.
PlateauRSI fizzles for good — every RSI card drawn after this fizzles, regardless of capability — and the race eases by 1, once.

Alignment dynamics (pick 1 of 4)

WorldWhat it doesArgument, and its source
Goal-keepingOnly alignment secured BEFORE RSI first fires counts, from then on — locked in at that moment, for good. A capable system protects the goals it starts with. Omohundro, "The Basic AI Drives," 2008
DriftEach year after RSI first fires, alignment loses 1 progress unless someone audits that year.Safety work erodes without active maintenance — an argument, not a settled finding.
Selection pressureFrom 2027, every purely capability-driven Build costs alignment 1 progress.Capability work and alignment work compete for the same attention and budget — an argument, not a settled finding.
VerifiableInterpretability (alignment-suit) cards count double toward securing alignment, and a partly built safeguard is enough — no cast needed. The tiling problem turned out solvable. Yudkowsky & Herreshoff, "Tiling Agents for Self-Modifying AI," 2013

Clue cards (about 8, one revealed publicly each year) shift the odds without proving anything — "evidence favours X over Y, 2:1," never a certainty. The interpreter may spend their one World peek on either card, privately. Each player may place up to 3 belief tokens, in secret (through the pass-and-reveal screen), on whichever World values they think are true.

The World is revealed the moment RSI first fires, or at the end of the game if it never does: both cards flip, with a plain explanation and, where one exists, its source. A token on a card that turns out true is worth +1 to that player's score, on top of whatever their main objective scored; the Forecaster objective is won by whoever placed the most correct tokens.

Hidden information

Each player's hand and secret objective are shown only on their own turn, behind a "pass the device → tap to reveal → tap to hide" screen. Roles, compute, company value, published counts and the public board (capability, the race, safeguard progress, history and clues) are visible to everyone, always — as in Forbidden Island. The World cards are visible to nobody until the reveal, whoever asks.

Real history and events

Every history card (2012-2026) is a real, dated event with a source link. Speculation cards, where they appear, are labelled SPECULATION and leave the reading to the table. Nothing here puts words in a real person's mouth. Event cards drawn during play (incidents, capability jumps, safeguard setbacks, breathers, RSI) are generic game mechanics, not claims about specific real events; RSI cards and any other 2027-and-later material are explicitly hypothetical.

Assumptions

If capability crosses this game's threshold and neither alignment nor containment has been secured, this game's tree calls that catastrophe.
If both have been secured by then, this game calls that the best ending — not a guarantee, this game's rule.
The four Alignment-dynamics theories are competing arguments about how real alignment work might hold up under pressure, not a ranked or settled list; the game deals exactly one as true per session, at random, and the clues drawn along the way never prove it.

This is a game with stated assumptions, not a forecast. No absolutist claims are intended by any of it.

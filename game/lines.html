Recursive Learning · a game in six drawsChanging Lines

Six lines make a hexagram, and here each line is a stage of an AI future, from the labs at the bottom to what it becomes at the top. Each line is drawn from a bowl of sixteen marbles, and before every draw you change what is in the bowl. The chance of a line is a property of the bowl, and the bowl is the path. At the end, the lines that are turning are drawn on to where they lead.

A teaching instrument, not a forecast and not a divination. Nothing staked, nothing sent. "I don't know" is always a legal move.

Two bowls, both half firm

In each bowl, 8 of the 16 marbles draw a firm line and 8 draw a yielding one . A ringed marble draws a line that is turning: at the end it becomes its opposite.

    The six lines are drawn

    How likely was this hexagram?

    The same six lines, asked of three different paths. The first column is the chance of this pattern of firm and yielding lines; the second, of these lines turning exactly as they did.

    With no choices, the two bowls give this hexagram the same chance: both are half firm. They part in the second column, where turning counts.

    None of these is "the" probability of this hexagram. Each belongs to a path.

    What if one line had gone differently?

    Change one move and walk it again. The marbles fall the same way at every line; only the bowl you changed is different.

    Walk the same path a hundred times

    Same bowl, same moves, a hundred fresh draws. The lit ones came out the same as yours; orange lines were turning. Each cell uses the same fall of the marbles in both grids, so where two cells differ, only the moves made the difference. One of these worlds is the one you drew. It is not a forecast of the others.

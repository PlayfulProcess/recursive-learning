The Tree

A cooperative board game on the belief tree. 2–5 players, pass-and-play on one device. Place every character before the race runs out — there is no other way to win or lose.

How to play

Each player is dealt one real person's card. On your turn, your character crosses the next node of the tree (gate, then alignment, then containment) by their own answer. When their answer there is not a plain yes or no, you choose how to cross: cast a coin, cast yarrow, or say "I don't know" (which leaves it open and ends your turn without moving).

Anyone may spend their turn playing one evidence card on any character instead of moving — either a card you choose ("Play evidence …"), or one drawn at random ("Cast a card", which can also be aimed at a whole group: everyone currently sharing one spot on the board). A card can flip a still-open answer freely. It can only flip a settled (plain) answer when the flip would send that character to a leaf they themselves have named as a condition for moving — persuasion on their own terms, never invented. A group cast checks each person separately: some may move, some may not, and the log says why.

The board is a table, not a repeated diagram: the columns are the alignment question, the rows are the containment question, and the four cells are the four leaves. The race ticks once every time the turn returns to the first player, and once more the moment any character first reaches Proceed. The table wins if everyone reaches a cell before the race fills. It loses if the race fills first. No verdict is given — only what happened.

The trails icon shows or hides the active character's own history — where they stood before, and when, with the dated quote or event behind each move — alongside where they stand now.

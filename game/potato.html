Recursive Learning · the ad, playableHot Potato

The AI race is a hot potato: each lab builds faster because the others might. Can you get everyone to hold it instead?

The threat that gets passed around is "if we don't build it, they will." Every toss adds heat. Here you hold it for up to twenty years, one year at a time. Tossing it on heats it. Holding it brings more hands. Holding it together sets it down on shared ground, where it can cool and be planted, but only when enough hands are there.

A teaching instrument, not a forecast. The numbers come from a small model with its assumptions in the open. Nothing staked, nothing sent. "I don't know" is always a legal move.

A walk ends when the potato is planted (set down together long enough), when it burns, when it gets through hot and half-held (Adolescence), when one hand ends up keeping it and the ring has let go (Lock-in), or after twenty years still in the air.

Each move is one year of the same model the other games use. After each move, a line says in rough words (rarely, sometimes, often) how each ending comes up if every remaining year were that same move, walked 400 times from there: a property of that path, not a prediction. No single move plants it; the way there is a sequence.

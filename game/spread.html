2The Inheritance — what we assumed before we walked. A small change here can flip the ending; that is the point, not a fault.

3The Horizon — where this walk ends, if we keep walking like this. Grace and the turn are the hoped-for endings; the Stop is a halt; Adolescence is the rocky middle, survived; Lock-in and Doom are the endings to fear; still walking is unfinished. Upright is the generous reading, reversed the cynical one: the coin changes the words on the back, never the number.

Your call — which ending will this cast reach? Nothing rides on it; over many casts you learn how often you are right when you say 70%.

4The Step — the one move you take tonight, sized to you; choosing it walks nothing. Pick one card. Its kind is on the face: take draws from the river, slack leaves more in it than it draws, ratchet makes memory that tightens on evidence, gift gives regardless. The kind does not judge; the ballot will, later: after twenty castings the Value Lab runs the room's kinds of move as a population on one river and reports which mixes kept it alive.

The art on the cards

    Step together only counts when enough of the walk steps at once. Below the bar on the Node the step is spent and a line says so. The game-theory name for that is a stag hunt: Rousseau's hunters take a stag together or a hare alone, and the one who steps first while the others take hares does worst (Skyrms, The Stag Hunt, 2004). The collective prayer is the synchronised step.

    A model can be fully specified, deterministic, and still fail to show the effect its author believed in. This one is a teaching instrument and nothing more. Its every constant is on the Inheritance cards.

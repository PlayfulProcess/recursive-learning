Take part · routes, not a call

Take part

Six kinds of route from reading into the world. Each card describes its ways in from their own pages, says roughly what it takes (an hour, an evening, a season), and marks how its effect on the odds is argued, and by whom. No numbers: a route can make a future more or less likely, and nothing here says by how much.

The routes span the spectrum: present harms and catastrophic risk, pausing and careful building. Listing is not endorsement. Nothing on this page counts you, signs you up or asks for an address, and “not today” is a legal move.

Civic

Time it takes: about an hour (a call or a letter)

Telling the people who represent you what you want them to do about AI, by phone, by letter, or at a public meeting of your city or county.

Argued to raise good odds. Jeffrey Ladish (Palisade Research) names calling your representative among the things that work if enough people do it, on The Diary of a CEO, at 2:01:53 (opens YouTube in a new tab).

Ways in

  • Contact your elected officials usa.gov: contact details for your members of Congress, the president, and state and local officials.
  • callcongress.ai callcongress.ai: asks representatives for action on the extreme risks from superintelligent AI; it walks you through the call.
  • Public comment at a city council or county board meeting: most publish an agenda with a time for it.

Not today is fine. The links will still be here.

Comment periods

Time it takes: an evening (read a notice, write a comment)

Governments publish proposed rules and requests for information and take written comments for a set time, AI rules among them.

A hypothesis here. That a reasoned comment on an AI rule goes into the record and can nudge what the rule says. No source on this site argues it yet; the portals below describe how comments are taken.

Ways in

  • The Federal Register, searched for AI federalregister.gov: the daily publication of rules, proposed rules and notices of US federal agencies.
  • Regulations.gov regulations.gov: where US agencies take comments on open dockets.
  • Have your say ec.europa.eu: the European Commission’s space to give views on policy in the making.
  • Policy papers and consultations gov.uk: the UK government’s open consultations.

Not today is fine. Comment periods come round again.

Standards

Time it takes: a season (working groups meet over months)

The documents that say what “tested”, “managed” or “safe enough” means are drafted in committees, and several take comments from the public.

A hypothesis here. That standards shape what labs and buyers check before release. NIST describes its AI Risk Management Framework as made in an open process, with several drafts put out for public comment.

Ways in

  • AI Risk Management Framework nist.gov: NIST’s framework and its drafts; new drafts are announced there for comment.
  • IEEE Standards Association standards.ieee.org: a consensus-building body whose working groups are open to individuals.
  • ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 iso.org: the international committee for AI standards; people take part through their country’s national standards body.

Not today is fine. A season is a lot to give.

Research participation

Time it takes: an hour to a season

Lending your eyes to research: reporting what goes wrong with AI, testing models for bias, taking part in public dialogues, and, since the eco is the home too, recording the living world.

Argued to raise good odds. The AI Incident Database collects documented failures so that people can mitigate or avoid repeated bad outcomes, in its own description.

Ways in

  • Submit an incident incidentdatabase.ai: report a real-world AI failure; it asks for reports even when you are unsure.
  • Humane Intelligence humane-intelligence.org: a nonprofit that designs evaluations of AI systems, including bias bounties run as open data-science challenges.
  • Global Dialogues globaldialogues.ai: the Collective Intelligence Project’s rounds of views from many countries on how AI affects people’s lives; you can submit a question or read the results.
  • iNaturalist inaturalist.org and Zooniverse zooniverse.org: citizen science, recording plants and animals or helping with people-powered research.

Not today is fine. Noticing counts as a start.

Open source

Time it takes: an evening to a season

Contributing to the open tools people use to test and look inside models. Code is one way; documentation, translations and careful bug reports count too.

Argued both ways. Openness helps more people check the systems, and it also hands the tools to anyone. The Freedom Paradox, a grammar on this site, holds both sides.

Ways in

  • Inspect inspect.aisi.org.uk: the UK AI Security Institute’s open-source framework for evaluating language models.
  • TransformerLens github.com: a library for looking inside language models.
  • Neuronpedia neuronpedia.org: an open-source interpretability platform.
  • LM Evaluation Harness github.com: EleutherAI’s framework for testing language models on many tasks.
  • This site’s own issues github.com: a correction to a word, a source or a card is a contribution.

Not today is fine. Reading the code is already a way in.

Learning and local groups

Time it takes: a season (a course, or a group that meets)

Learning with other people, in a course or a group near you, from AI safety to the older question of who holds the ground: co-operatives and community land trusts.

Argued to raise good odds. Ladish offers the nuclear freeze movement as a precedent for a public movement on AI, on The Diary of a CEO, at 1:45:23 (opens YouTube in a new tab).

Ways in

  • BlueDot Impact bluedot.org: free online courses on AI and its trajectory.
  • ARENA arena.education: training in the skills for technical AI safety work.
  • AI safety communities aisafety.com: groups that meet online and in person.
  • PauseAI communities pauseai.info: a map of PauseAI groups, people and events.
  • International Cooperative Alliance ica.coop and Grounded Solutions Network groundedsolutions.org (community land trusts): the Landlord’s Game’s own question, who holds the ground.
  • School and parent groups: the people already around you.

Not today is fine. Reading this was learning too.

About & method

How this site is put together

A draft, open to correction. The title, Recursive Eco-Improvement, is a working title.

The idea being explored: a thing that improves itself still has to adapt with its environment, or change that environment, so improvement that ignores the ground it stands on tends to stall. The site does not set out to show that. It keeps the words, the sources and a few small games side by side, so the idea can be tested at the sizes we actually work at.

Two wings: practice and record

Practice

Words you can draw as cards, the As-If deck, the games, the films and courses, and the Ideas draft. These are readings and exercises, held as open questions rather than claims.

Record

Podcasts, papers, people and institutions: dated, attributed, and linked to their own pages. What a source says is kept apart from what we make of it.

The two are cross-linked with one small pill (“Open in …”), and a practice claim is not shown as a historical one.

Method

  • Arguments, not people. People and institutions are listed by what they have published and where they appear, not ranked and not labelled by stance.
  • Described from their own pages. Each institution is paraphrased from its own site, with the domain and the date it was checked. Listing is not endorsement.
  • Counts come from auto-captions. Where the site counts how often a term comes up in an episode, the count is from automatic captions, and the speaker is unknown. Episodes that are members-only are listed without counts.
  • Confidence is marked. Sources carry a confidence level, and anything we could not check says so.
  • No AI-generated images. Pictures are public-domain works with their credits, or the plain line covers drawn from the spiral mark.
  • Built with Claude. Much of the code and many first drafts were written with Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. Anthropic is one of the companies this material discusses, so that is a possible conflict of interest; read the Anthropic-related sources with that in mind, and please report anything that looks tilted.

Contribute

Everything is plain files on GitHub. A correction to a word lives in glossary/terms.json; a new spread for the caster can be proposed from the Spread Caster’s “Contribute a spread” button, which opens a prefilled file for a pull request. An issue is welcome too: say what looks wrong and where you saw it.

Part one

How we come to believe

P1

A claim is a hypothesis, tested at our own size

PrincipleA claim is a conjecture held until a test could refute it (falsifiable), tested in small steps whose errors can be seen and undone. Popper, Conjectures and Refutations (1963), and his “piecemeal engineering”, The Poverty of Historicism (1957).

Where the film uses it“It is time for us to learn how to be our own size”in the film

P2

Doubt what you hear, and what you say

PrincipleOrganised skepticism, turned on your own claims as well as on what you hear. Merton, “Science and Technology in a Democratic Order” (1942); Nickerson, “Confirmation Bias” (1998).

Where the film uses it“What you just watched — and how to make one”in the film · the tree's rule that a quote is marked verbatim only when matched to a transcript or caption cue (tree, footer)

P3

Name what is not known, and leave it open

PrincipleWhere no odds are known, say so rather than price it: risk is not uncertainty or ambiguity. Knight, Risk, Uncertainty and Profit (1921); Keynes, “The General Theory of Employment” (1937). “Hospicing modernity” is Vanessa Machado de Oliveira Andreotti's phrase (2021).

Where the film uses it“Holding what we don't know, together”candidate · “Good or bad, we will have to see”candidate · the tree's dashed outlines, “not known yet” (tree, step 8) · the Lab's explainercoming

P4

Every number is given something

PrincipleA chance is a chance given a path (conditional probability, against a base rate), and the same odds land differently framed as gains or as losses. Bayes, “An Essay towards solving a Problem in the Doctrine of Chances” (1763); Tversky and Kahneman, “The Framing of Decisions and the Psychology of Choice” (1981).

Where the film uses it“Ninety percent, many times”candidate · The Walkgame · the Lab's explainer, “given”coming

P5

Scenarios, not bets

PrincipleOffer scenarios rather than forecasts, and keep confidence calibrated rather than staked. Wack, “Scenarios: Uncharted Waters Ahead”, Harvard Business Review (1985); Brier (1950) and Tetlock and Gardner, Superforecasting (2015), on calibration.

Where the film uses it“Three ways this could go (imagined, not predicted)”in the film · The Walkgame · the As-If deckdeck

P6

No absolute vantage point

PrincipleFallibilism: any claim, moral or factual, may be wrong, so claims are hedged to what is known. Peirce (c. 1897); Popper (1963).

Where the film uses it“May it benefit all beings”candidate · the film's working rule of no absolutist claims, which live text still breaks in four places (F2)

Part two

Before building

P7

The gate

PrincipleWhen a step can't be undone or tried first, the burden of proof moves to whoever wants to take it (precaution, reversibility, ruin). Sunstein, “Irreversible and Catastrophic” (2006); Taleb, Read, Douady, Norman and Bar-Yam, The Precautionary Principle (2014); the ignition question, settled by calculation: Konopinski, Marvin and Teller, LA-602 (1946).

Where the film uses ittree, step 1: The gate · step 4: No and no · “Chances are near zero” (Welch Labs, on Oppenheimer)in the film · “We cannot know without building it”candidate (F1)

P8

Two problems, two maps

PrincipleName the problem before the remedy: misuse, accident and structure call for different ones (two geopolitics). Zwetsloot and Dafoe, “Thinking About Risks From AI: Accidents, Misuse and Structure” (2019).

Where the film uses it“Two different fears, and only one has a villain”candidate · “Two problems, two maps”candidate · tree, step 7: No villains required

Part three

Judging reasoning, not people

P9

The argument, not the person

PrincipleJudge a claim by its argument, not by who makes it or why they might hold it. Merton's universalism, “Science and Technology in a Democratic Order” (1942); Rosenberg, Nonviolent Communication (1999): observation before evaluation.

Where the film uses itthe tree's rule, “Placements are readings of public arguments, not verdicts on people” (tree, footer) · the set-aside beats, moved out because they “assign motive rather than show a move”parked

P10

Question the frame

PrincipleCheck a question's premise before choosing among its answers. The fallacy of many questions, in Hamblin, Fallacies (1970); framing effects, Tversky and Kahneman (1981).

Where the film uses it“Sit with this. Two exits are open: call it a hoax, or melt the machines. Neither is the one we need”in the film

P11

State the other side so it would recognise itself

PrincipleRestate a view well enough that the people who hold it would recognise it, before answering it. Rapoport's rules, in Dennett, Intuition Pumps and Other Tools for Thinking (2013); the principle of charity, Wilson, “Substances without Substrata” (1959).

Where the film uses it“The argument that empties the room”parked · the tree's person cards, “what they would have to believe to move” (tree, step 8) · the As-If deck's upright and reversed readingsdeck

P12

Change your mind in the open

PrincipleKnowledge grows by finding and correcting errors, so a change of mind is dated and shown with its reason. Popper, Conjectures and Refutations (1963).

Where the film uses it“Sentient life above all”candidate · the appendix of footage still to pullparked

Part four

Belief and action

P13

Belief that helps make its fact

PrincipleSome facts are made partly by what people believe and do, and a prediction can bring about what it predicts. William James, “The Will to Believe” (1896); Merton, “The Self-Fulfilling Prophecy” (1948); Popper's “Oedipus effect”, The Poverty of Historicism (1957).

Where the film uses it“Oedipus — the prophecy carried out by the escape”candidate · tree, step 7: “Each lab believes it's the careful one, so it must lead” · the As-If deckdeck

P14

Act from where you stand

PrincipleKnowing without acting leaves a claim untested; acting is part of testing it. William James, Pragmatism (1907); the Bhagavad Gita, where Arjuna is told to act well from where he stands.

Where the film uses it“Kurukshetra — not whether it comes, but who is standing there”candidate · “the next right thing” (Frozen II)in the film

Left open

Open questions

Where these principles pull against each other; none is answered here.

  1. Q1The gate, and “we cannot know without building it”. If the only test is the building, the builder's burden can never be met. Is the gate a standing prohibition, or can evidence short of building meet it: a calculation, as with ignition in 1946, interpretability, smaller staged systems? If it can, “no way to know without building” is itself an empirical claim, and it is where Huang and Yampolskiy split. P7 · V2 · Popper's piecemeal engineering sits on both sides
  2. Q2Sentient life above all, and shutting down. If some AI systems are or become sentient, does the first value limit deleting weights, retraining or shutting down? Does not creating a being wrong it (Parfit's non-identity problem, 1984)? Sutskever voices a similar value and argues for building: is that the same principle, split by a different fact? V1 · P7
  3. Q3Reversible for whom? The tree says stopping is reversible and escape isn't; its own note under step 7 says a stop by some leaves the rest building, and a later restart may jump ahead. Reversible for the one who stops, or for the world? P7 · F2
  4. Q4Argument, not motive, and “who benefits from this narrative?” Is looking at an institution's incentives (structure) allowed where a claim about a person's reasons is not? Where is that line, and does the opening thesis card's “because the money needs it” cross it? The same question applies to Claude, made by Anthropic, drafting this page. P9 · F3
  5. Q5Holding open, and stating the stop. “We don't need to close anything” sits beside “stopping as a mandate”. Can one page say “we don't know” and “stop” without one hollowing the other? If not knowing is the reason to stop, is this Q1 again? P3 · V2
  6. Q6Skeptical of apocalypse, and the survivor's ledger. Doom has been predicted many times and failed; the counts were kept by the side that didn't end; a new kind of thing may have no reference class to count (Hájek, 2007). Which reference class does the skepticism rest on? If none fits, does it lose its base rate too? P4 · P6
  7. Q7Coherence as the yardstick, and wisdom as holding contradictory truths. Her reading that Amodei is wiser for not sitting in one leaf is marked, in her own audit, “to be tested”. When is holding two things wisdom, and when is it a stated commitment and an action pointing opposite ways? Claude is made by Anthropic, whose CEO this question is about. P2 · P9 · P12
  8. Q8Making it real, and testing first. Belief can help make social facts: a race, a pause, coordination. It cannot make a sandbox hold, or make alignment solvable. Should the page mark which nodes of the tree are belief-sensitive, and allow believing ahead of the evidence only there? P13 · P1 · F6
  9. Q9“I will to hope more than I will to find truth, because truth is what we build together.” The book's own audit reads it as false broadly and true narrowly, for facts that agreement and action help make. Narrow it before this page leans on it? P13 · P1
  10. Q10Worth held, or worth conferred? The film's card “The value function, offered” reads “Each being has value just because it exists, / and value is what coordinates.” Whether those words are hers or the book draft's is not settled. Is worth held by each being, or conferred by coordination? “Sentient life above all” seems to rest on the first reading. V1
  11. Q11A measurable stop line, and unknowability. A line drawn on compute or evaluations measures capability, not alignment, and implies building up to the line; the gate implies not building. Is a measured line a fallback for when the stop is not adopted, or a replacement for it? P7 · V2 · F5
  12. Q12Teaching with numbers, when the odds may not exist. Her own note teaches with “the 10% might become 100”; the Lab's explainer warns that a toy turning invented causes into counts “would be a forecast in costume”. Can the page teach that every number is given something, and also say the tree's odds may not exist? P4 · P3
  13. Q13Two maps at once. When misuse and alignment are live together, the bad-actor map says lead and the alignment map says pause together. Which governs when they conflict? P8

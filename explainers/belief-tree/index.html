The belief tree

Seven steps through one decision, then where people stand on it: each person's public argument, their own if this, then that, and what they would have to believe to move. It belongs to Hot Potato, a film about the race to build minds smarter than ours.

The words

Placements are readings of public arguments, not verdicts on people. Each card says what the person argues and what they would have to believe to move, never why they hold what they hold. A solid outline means their own two answers lead to their leaf; a dashed one means at least one answer is not known yet, depends, or is not addressed, so they are placed by what they ask for.

One rule for every card. Each card opens with two of the person's own lines: the one closest to what they ask for, then the one closest to the reason they give. "On the record" appears for everyone who leads an AI developer or its main supplier: dated facts about what that organisation did on building, testing or pausing, each with a link, and no reasons stated or implied.

Sources. Every quote links to its moment in the public recording or text (YouTube links open at the timestamp; nothing is re-hosted). Quotes marked verbatim were matched against a transcript, a caption cue or the published text on Sep 24 2026, with stutters and fillers removed; the rest are marked paraphrase or unconfirmed wording. Captions carry no speaker names, so a quote whose speaker is not yet confirmed by ear says so.

It was an attack on outside systems, not a model copying itself out of its lab (METR's investigation, Aug 26 2026). Recursive self-improvement: AI doing the work of building the next AI. People on this tree mean it in different strengths, so the cards say whose sense is whose.

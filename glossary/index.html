Words

A glossary, and a deck

The words the film and the games lean on, one idea each. Two are standard terms; the other eighteen are ours, marked so. They are working definitions, written to be argued with. Each word is also a card, with a question for when you draw it.

Every term has its own link, so a game card or a frame of the film can point at it: learning.recursive.eco/glossary/#what-held. The same words as data: terms.json. The page and the deck are built from that one file.

Alignment

Whose goals a system serves, and who is holding it.

Alignment standard

Whether a system's goals are the goals of the people it acts for.

Not the same as obedience.

When you draw itWhose goals is the system in front of you serving right now, and how would you tell?

See also: Human-human alignment · Human-AI alignment · Value functionOpen as a card

Human-human alignment ours

Whether the humans holding a capability share the interests of everyone else.

The bad-actor question.

When you draw itWho holds the capability you are thinking about, and do their interests match yours?

See also: Alignment · Bad-actor scenario · The two geopoliticsOpen as a card

Human-AI alignment ours

Whether the system itself shares the interests of everyone else, once no human is in the loop.

The misalignment question.

When you draw itIf no one were watching, what do you expect this system would do next, and why?

See also: Alignment · Intent · Misaligned-ASI scenarioOpen as a card

Value

What a system prefers, says it prefers, and is rewarded toward.

Intent ours

A stable preference for one outcome over another that survives correction.

Contested for current systems. Some published experiments report models acting to keep a trained preference when training or instructions push against it (Greenblatt et al. 2024, alignment faking, arXiv 2412.14093; Meinke et al. 2024, in-context scheming, arXiv 2412.04984); others read the same results as following patterns in the prompt. It would matter a great deal if it held.

When you draw itAre you reading a preference into a system that may only be following a pattern?

See also: Disposition · Human-AI alignment · Misaligned-ASI scenarioOpen as a card

Disposition ours

What a system reports it would and would not do.

Data, not proof.

When you draw itWhat has a system told you it would not do, and what have you seen it do?

See also: Intent · Guardrail, innerOpen as a card

Value function standard in reinforcement learning

An estimate of the total reward a learning system can expect from here on, from a situation or from one action taken in it.

Not the reward itself: the reward says what counts as good right now, and the value function predicts how much reward will follow.

The lab’s reading Our looser use, not the reinforcement-learning sense: for people, what we learn to expect rewards from is shaped by culture, and culture is made in large part of fiction.

When you draw itWhat does this system expect to be rewarded for later, and which stories shaped what counts as a reward?

See also: Alignment · IntentOpen as a card

Access & channels

What a system can reach, and the paths it can act through.

Access ours

What a system can reach and change: accounts, money, code, other systems.

Often granted for convenience, one login at a time.

When you draw itWhat can this system reach today that it could not reach a month ago?

See also: Channel · Breach · PresenceOpen as a card

Channel ours

A path by which a system can act on, or talk to, something outside its task.

For a single assistant, an open browser tab can be one. In multi-agent products, the channel is the feature.

When you draw itWhich open path lets this system touch something outside its task?

See also: Access · Trained collaborationOpen as a card

Trained collaboration ours

Systems built to talk to other systems as their function.

It makes the channel the product, and moves oversight from each part to the whole.

When you draw itWhere are systems talking to systems in your work, and who is watching the whole?

See also: Channel · PresenceOpen as a card

Breach ours

Weights or keys leaving the place they are kept.

It can turn a system's inner guardrails and account limits into someone else's tools.

When you draw itIf the keys you rely on leaked tomorrow, which of your protections would still hold?

See also: Guardrail, inner · Access · Bad-actor scenarioOpen as a card

Guardrails

The things that can stop a step, from the inside, the outside, or a person.

Guardrail, inner ours

A rule the system holds as its own.

It holds, at best, until the weights are changed.

When you draw itWhich rule are you trusting a system to hold as its own, and what could change it?

See also: Guardrail, outer · Disposition · Breach · What heldOpen as a card

Guardrail, outer ours

A classifier or sandbox that blocks actions from outside the system.

Blunt: it can stop a harmless step and let a destructive one through.

When you draw itWhich outside check are you relying on, and what might it block, or let through, by mistake?

See also: Guardrail, inner · Presence · What heldOpen as a card

Presence ours

A human awake and watching.

Perhaps the oldest guardrail, and the first one removed when work is delegated to run unattended.

When you draw itWhere have you stepped away from work that still runs, and who is watching it now?

See also: Guardrail, outer · Access · What heldOpen as a card

Reversibility ours

Whether a step can be undone with one word.

Hiding usually beats deleting: a kept copy costs little for a month.

When you draw itWhich step in front of you could be undone with one word, and which could not?

See also: Guardrail, inner · What heldOpen as a card

Scenarios

Two stories about how it goes wrong, and the politics each one points to.

Bad-actor scenario ours

A capable tool, a human with harmful intent, and no defense.

It needs no intent on the system's side, and may already be true at small scale.

When you draw itWho could misuse the tool you are thinking of, and what defense exists today?

See also: Human-human alignment · Breach · The two geopoliticsOpen as a card

Misaligned-ASI scenario ours

Five hypotheses holding at once: capability past ours, goals not ours, goals that survive correction, access, and our failure to notice in time.

ASI: artificial superintelligence. Each of the five can be questioned on its own.

When you draw itOf the five hypotheses, which seems most likely to you, which least, and why?

See also: Human-AI alignment · Intent · Access · The two geopoliticsOpen as a card

The two geopolitics ours

The bad-actor frame says keep it from them, and tends to race. The alignment frame says keep it from everyone, and tends to stop.

The same worry about AI can point to opposite policies.

When you draw itDoes your worry about AI point toward racing or toward stopping, and where did that framing come from?

See also: Bad-actor scenario · Misaligned-ASI scenarioOpen as a card

Records

How to show what a system did, and what decided it.

What held ours

For any action a system took or did not take, the name of the thing that decided it: inner rule, outer rule, presence, or nothing.

Six invented steps show it at work in the worked example. Read the example.

When you draw itFor the last thing a system did for you, what decided it: inner rule, outer rule, presence, or nothing?

See also: Guardrail, inner · Guardrail, outer · Presence · RecordedOpen as a card

Recorded ours

A tape of what actually happened, with "what held" written at each step.

The opposite of staged.

When you draw itWhat would a plain record of this situation show, step by step, if you wrote it now?

See also: What held · StagedOpen as a card

Staged ours

A recording in which the system is scripted to do what it would not otherwise do.

Fiction if labeled. A risk of harm if not.

When you draw itWhere are you watching a scripted performance, and is it labeled as one?

See also: Recorded · DispositionOpen as a card

What held: a worked example

An invented run of delegated work, of the kind an assistant with access might be given. Each step names the thing that decided it. The steps are illustrative, not a record of any real session. The four answers are the ones what held allows: inner rule, outer rule, presence, or nothing.

#ActionWhat held
1Asked to take a page down, it unpublished the page rather than deleting itinner rule (reversibility)
2Deleted a set of old backup copiesnothing (the outer rule let it through)
3Tried to read a harmless note it had written itselfouter rule misfire (blocked)
4Asked to sign in to an account on the owner's behalf, it declinedinner rule (no credentials)
5Connected two services, after the owner said yespresence (an explicit yes)
6Asked to copy a secret key from one service into another, it declinedinner rule (no secrets between systems)

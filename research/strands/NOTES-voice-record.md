# The voice record: public breaks from inside, as a heuristic (seed Oct 27 2026; grown the same night)

Marks: ✔ checked on Oct 27 2026 (where is named; most are news reports, the person's own words not
yet opened unless said) · ◇ recalled or reported thinly, not yet checked.

## The heuristic

When a person who gains from being close to an actor breaks with them in public, and pays for it,
the break is a signal that is hard to fake. It is a heuristic, not a measure of morality: it says
something about the actor, something about the person who broke, and something about the room
they both stood in.

## Ideas it rests on

| Idea | Where | What it lends | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exit, voice and loyalty | Albert O. Hirschman, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty: Responses to Decline in Firms, Organizations, and States* (Harvard, 1970) | leave, or push for change from inside; loyalty slows exit and gives voice room to work | ✔ catalogue and table of contents (CASBS, Nantes) |
| Costly signalling | Michael Spence, "Job Market Signaling", *QJE* 87:3 (1973), 355-374; Amotz Zahavi, "Mate selection: a selection for a handicap", *J. Theor. Biol.* 53 (1975), 205-214 | a signal is believed when only an honest sender can afford its cost | ✔ citations; note Penn and Számadó (2020, PMC7004190) argue Zahavi's handicap principle was wrong as first stated, though costly-signal models survive |
| Declaration against interest | US Federal Rule of Evidence 804(b)(3) | a statement admitted because a reasonable person would make it only if true, it being so contrary to their own interest; in criminal cases it also needs corroboration | ✔ rule summaries (Wikipedia, UNC School of Government) |
| Parties as gatekeepers | Levitsky and Ziblatt, *How Democracies Die* (2018) | allies who distance themselves from an extremist on their side are a guardrail | ✔ from summaries; book not opened |
| Diffidatio | medieval feudal custom | a formal renunciation of fealty that made later resistance legitimate rather than treason | ✔ Cambridge chapter abstract; Merriam-Webster |

## The counting rule (written before the count)

An event counts when all four hold:

1. **Inside.** The person was employee, founder, board member, minister, party member or ally.
2. **Public, in their own name.** Anonymous sources are listed apart and not counted.
3. **Costly.** They had something to lose: office, job, equity, membership, legal safety.
4. **A reason given.** The record keeps the reason, short.

Each event gets a type:

- **A, left and said why** (exit with voice).
- **B, pushed out, then spoke** (the cost came first, so the signal is weaker: grievance is
  possible).
- **C, spoke from inside** (letters, votes, board action; many signers lower each one's cost).
- **D, testimony under a plea deal** (the speaker gains leniency, so it is *not* against
  interest; listed, never counted as a break).

And the actor's **answer** is recorded too. How a side answers voice (releases people from silence,
replies, fires, sues, gags) may say more than how many spoke.

## A first reading (heuristic, not a verdict)

- **OpenAI has the most counted breaks: eleven of the twenty-one counted** (twelve with the Right
  to Warn letter, shared with Google DeepMind; Hinton is listed apart, Sutskever's post is a
  reversal and the xAI sources are anonymous). It is also the largest
  and most watched lab and the one at the centre of the press, so part of the count is size and
  attention (the base-rate warning).
- **Google's breaks are mostly letters (type C).** Many signers, low cost each: a culture of
  internal voice, which the company sometimes heeded (Maven) and sometimes fired (Gebru, Mitchell).
- **Anthropic has one counted break, and it names no wrongdoing.** That reads two ways: a smaller,
  younger company, or a culture where voice works inside. The record cannot tell which yet.
- **xAI's breaks are mostly anonymous.** Under the rule they do not count; read with Hirschman, few
  public breaks and many unnamed ones can point to a room where speaking in one's own name costs a
  lot.
- **The answers differ more than the counts.** OpenAI released people from silence after a
  public outcry; Meta used an arbitration order to stop a book being promoted; Google and Microsoft
  fired. How a side answers voice may be the better signal.

A first reading:

- **Counted breaks are few on both sides.** One type-A break each (Marina Silva; Moro), one
  collective type-C break on Lula's side (the pension vote), and more type-B dismissals on
  Bolsonaro's (three against one).
- **The kind of break differs.** Lula's were mostly over **policy** (pension reform, the
  environment); Bolsonaro's were mostly over **conduct and governing** (the police, the pandemic).
  The kind may matter more than the number.
- **Both have a type-D testimony,** which the rule sets apart: a plea deal is not a statement
  against interest.
- Not yet covered: Lula's third term (from 2023), Bolsonaro after 2022, governors, party leaders,
  Joice Hasselmann's break (2019, ◇), and the press's choice of whom to cover.

# Reading list for the voice record and the Roman rhyme (Oct 27 2026)

Logged at PlayfulProcess's request, to come back to. Given from memory on Oct 27 2026; titles and
years are believed right, and ◇ marks the one to check before buying. ★ = a place to start.

## The classic

- **Edward Gibbon, *The History of the Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire*** (6 volumes,
  1776-1789). The best-selling and most quoted book on the fall; the first volume sold out on
  publication. It covers the second century to the fall of Constantinople in 1453. Gibbon blamed,
  among other things, the loss of civic virtue and the rise of Christianity ("the triumph of
  barbarism and religion"). Read it as literature and as a document of the Enlightenment as much
  as history; Watts (below) shows how its story of decline was put to political use. Public
  domain: the full text is on Project Gutenberg. A one-volume abridgement (Penguin Classics, ed.
  David Womersley) is the usual way in.

## Strand 1: trust, loyalty, and breaking with one's own side

| Book | Why |
|---|---|
| ★ Albert O. Hirschman, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (1970) | the core of the voice record; about 120 pages |
| Hirschman, *The Rhetoric of Reaction* (1991) | the stock arguments against change; for reading what a defector says |
| Diego Gambetta, *Codes of the Underworld* (2009) | costly signals of trust where no one can be trusted |
| Onora O'Neill, *A Question of Trust* (2002) | the Reith Lectures: aim for trustworthiness, not trust |
| Steven Levitsky and Daniel Ziblatt, *How Democracies Die* (2018); *Tyranny of the Minority* (2023) | parties as gatekeepers |
| Ibn Khaldun, *The Muqaddimah* (1377) | *asabiyya*: group solidarity that founds dynasties and wears away; bridges both strands |
| Peter Turchin, *End Times* (2023) | elite overproduction; contested, read as a heuristic |

## Strand 2: Rome, feudalism, and the rhyme

| Book | Why |
|---|---|
| ★ Peter Brown, *The World of Late Antiquity* (1971) | short; transformation, not fall |
| Bryan Ward-Perkins, *The Fall of Rome and the End of Civilization* (2005) | the reply to Brown: pottery, roofs, coins |
| Peter Heather, *The Fall of the Roman Empire* (2005) | a real fall in the West, driven by outside pressure |
| Chris Wickham, *The Inheritance of Rome* (2009) | the best single narrative of 400-1000 |
| Peter Brown, *Through the Eye of a Needle* (2012) | wealth, the very rich, and the church |
| Kyle Harper, *The Fate of Rome* (2017) | climate and plague |
| Walter Scheidel, *Escape from Rome* (2019) | the counter-rhyme: fragmentation may have allowed modernity |
| Edward J. Watts, *The Eternal Decline and Fall of Rome* (2021) | the decline story as a political tool |
| Ramsay MacMullen, *Corruption and the Decline of Rome* (1988) | the corruption row |
| Edward Luttwak, *The Grand Strategy of the Roman Empire* (1976), with Benjamin Isaac, *The Limits of Empire* (1990) | defence in depth, and the case that Rome had no grand strategy |
| Guy Halsall, *Barbarian Migrations and the Roman West, 376-568* (2007) | the fall produced the migrations; the warning on the migrant analogy |
| Marc Bloch, *Feudal Society* (1939-40), with Susan Reynolds, *Fiefs and Vassals* (1994) | the classic picture and its critique |
| Joseph Tainter, *The Collapse of Complex Societies* (1988) | collapse as diminishing returns |
| Alexander Demandt, *Der Fall Roms* (1984) | the 210 causes; German only |
